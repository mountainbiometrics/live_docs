## Additions
- [[20260625221953]]
  User's reiterated methodology for live_docs: treat docs like maintainable code; capture the why (decision process, use-cases, constraints) not the what; clean liberally to show what is not what was; living != implemented; the decision type is for architectural decisions only and must be scoped; a what without a why is repaired or removed; and the belongs_to tree should be roughly balanced (~5 deep x ~5 wide).
- [[20260625222613]]
  live_docs exists to capture the why — the decision process, the use-cases a plan set out to solve, the constraints that bounded it, the requirements it had to meet — not the what the code already encodes. The why outlives any implementation; recreating the what from the why is a failed pass.
- [[20260625222626]]
  Design knowledge is maintained like well-factored code: decomposed into atomic, single-responsibility docs that can be read, revised, superseded, or deleted one at a time. Atomicity is what makes a doc precisely editable, the opposite of conventional documentation's replace-the-whole-blob model.
- [[20260625222638]]
  Every doc except a pure signpost must carry its why — the rationale, constraint, use-case, or decision it serves. A doc that states only a what, with no why, is a defect: repair it (add the why, citing provenance) or remove it. This is the operational enforcement of capture-the-why.
- [[20260625222655]]
  The decision type is reserved for deliberate architectural choices — a choice among alternatives, with a rationale, that future work shouldn't re-decide — scoped via belongs_to to the level it binds. A decision that merely asserts a thing exists ('have a module that does X') is a component; an operational choice belongs scoped under the component it governs.
- [[20260625222711]]
  The belongs_to hierarchy should be a roughly balanced tree — neither too tall nor too wide. Heuristic target: ~5 levels deep with ~5 children per signpost, not ~20-deep/2-wide nor ~4-deep/10-wide. It should generally mirror code structure but is a hybrid with the system's mental model, since docs explain the system, not just the codebase.
- [[20260625222723]]
  The distinctive value of gardening — and of reading the codebase through the living docs — is evaluative: judging when something is idiotic, redundant, or no longer earning its place. Cataloguing what exists is the low-value part; deciding what should stop existing is the high-value part, and the lever against intention-lost residue.
