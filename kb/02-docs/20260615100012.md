## Context

When a cascade check runs after a doc change, the check must produce a verdict for each dependent. The vocabulary and logging behavior of those verdicts need to be defined.

## Decision

Per dependent, cascade emits one of four verdicts: `inconsequential` | `cascade` | `incompatible` | `context-request`. Every verdict is recorded regardless of type. Propagation stops at `inconsequential`. `context-request` is used when the judge cannot confidently determine impact, and the walk continues after the question is answered or the gap is named. A neighbor at or below the change's intent is `cascade`. `incompatible` names a neighbor that states the same claim at a higher intent than the change carries, or a frozen neighbor: its assertion stays and the new claim is written beside it ([[20261006190940]]).

## Consequences

`inconsequential` as the explicit norm (rather than "no verdict") prevents silent false-negatives. `context-request` guards against the worst failure mode: a judge that quietly marks things inconsequential when it lacks the context to know (P2). Recording all verdicts creates an audit trail that gardening passes can mine to detect patterns (P6 — the system maintains itself). `incompatible` is part of that record. It does not stop the other writes. `cascade` propagates to dependents of dependents.
