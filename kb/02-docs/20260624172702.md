## Statement

A knowledge base requires two independent structural properties, both of which must be maintained:

1. **Placement correctness (doc-level):** every doc has a `belongs_to` that is semantically accurate — the doc genuinely belongs under that parent, and the edge is not misleading.
2. **Tree navigability (branch-level):** every signpost is right-sized — the number of direct children is small enough that a reader can scan and orient without being overwhelmed.

A signpost with 100 direct children is a navigability failure even if every child's placement is individually correct. Being 'in the tree' is necessary but not sufficient.

## Why these are independent

Placement correctness is a property of the edge between a doc and its parent. Navigability is a property of a signpost's fan-out. A massive branch can consist entirely of correctly-placed docs — the failure is at the branch level, not the individual-doc level. Conversely, a small branch can contain badly-placed docs. Fixing one does not fix the other.

## What maintenance looks like

- **Placement errors** → re-home the mis-placed doc to the correct signpost.
- **Overgrown branches** → introduce intermediate signposts when children cluster into sub-themes, and re-home children one level down. This is the structural analogue of decompose: just as a doc with two reasons to change should be split, a signpost whose children cluster into sub-themes should gain sub-signposts.
