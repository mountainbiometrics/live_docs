## Context

Gardening — the maintenance discipline that enforces single-responsibility, catches drift, repairs structure, and normalizes form — was originally a single monolithic pass. But it conflates several independent concerns (the size/ownership of a doc, where a doc lives, and its surface quality), and a monolith cannot be tuned one concern at a time.

## Decision

Gardening is realized as a **thin dispatcher over a family of single-purpose phases**, the same composition shape skills-compose prescribes for any orchestrator. The dispatcher is the sole entry point and **owns the whole episode**: the single START timestamp, the routing decision (which phase(s) the store needs), the one cascade-check over the *union* of every doc the phases changed, and the one review record. Each phase does exactly one job, applies its own writes, and **reports the ids it changed** — it captures no START, runs no cascade, and emits no review. Episode bookkeeping belongs to the dispatcher alone; the phases are pure, independently-runnable, individually-tunable units.

The dispatcher accepts **natural-language intent** — 'clean up duplicates', 'find homes for orphans', 'fix the cut-off titles' — and reasons from that intent to the appropriate phase(s). Sub-skills are never user-addressable; the user addresses only the dispatcher. This keeps internal phase structure from leaking into the user interface.

## Consequences

- Each concern becomes separately tunable: a phase can be invoked, thresholds adjusted, and behavior refined without disturbing the others.
- **Antagonist phases are ordered by the dispatcher, not left to collide.** Splitting overloaded docs and merging thin/duplicate ones pull in opposite directions, so a full sweep runs the splitting phase *before* the merging phase, and merging must never fold anything that would re-create a split candidate.
- **Detecting that a dependency changed after its dependent is not a gardening write-pass.** That belief belongs to cascade-check, which already owns it; its read-only kernel survives only as a *routing signal* the dispatcher uses to decide where to send work — not as a phase that writes.
- **Sub-skills are not user-invocable, not by design.** The non-user-invocable flag prevents direct invocation from the slash-list, but more importantly, sub-skills are not designed as entry points. The dispatcher is the only coherent entry point because it owns the episode; a sub-skill invoked directly would capture no START, no cascade, and emit no review — the episode would be invisible to the change-trail.
- One cascade and one review per episode keep the change-trail coherent no matter how many phases ran.
