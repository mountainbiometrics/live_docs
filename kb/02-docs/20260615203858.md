## Context

idea_dump identified a need to disambiguate between 'a coding principle that is useful in general' vs. 'a coding principle that is vital for our current architecture' — a relevance or generalizedness axis. This is distinct from the existing adoption axes: `level` (incidental → trial → preference → requirement) tracks how settled/decided a thing is, and `state` (actual | target) tracks temporal position. Neither directly encodes 'how narrowly scoped to our current situation is this?'

## Decision

A third metadata axis for relevance/generalizedness is deferred. The `level` field is the current proxy: `requirement` implies a strong, specific necessity for the current system, while `incidental` implies an emerged-but-open-to-challenge pattern that may be general or narrow. If the distinction becomes a pain point (e.g. principles that are broadly useful get confused with those that are highly context-specific), a `scope` tag or a new `generalizedness` field can be introduced. This decision should be revisited when live_docs is adopted by a second team or repo.

## Consequences

No new field is added now. The open question is tracked here rather than in raw notes. If `scope` tags on principle/decision docs are used with care, they partially satisfy the use case: a principle tagged `scope: [AnnotatorAgent]` is clearly narrower than one tagged `scope: [live_docs]`.
