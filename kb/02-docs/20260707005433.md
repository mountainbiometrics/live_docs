Comparing live_docs to neighboring agent-documentation tools — a code->docs generator, a rule enforcer, and retrieval/MCP bolt-ons — reinforces its existing principles and surfaces two worth recording explicitly. (1) Cascade repairs drift by SURGICALLY REGENERATING the stale atomic node — rewriting that one doc to current truth from the intent-graph, not flagging it and not wholesale-regenerating from code; atomicity is what makes the local rewrite safe, and the result is a living doc, re-derived rather than snapshotted. (2) The system's substrate is knowledge CURRENCY — keeping knowledge true as reality changes, a write-time concern — while retrieval, context-injection, and harness integration (RAG, MCP) are downstream, swappable, read-time layers left out of scope; being unopinionated about the harness is a consequence of that boundary, not a gap. The remaining comparison insights — recoverability of the what vs. the why, docs->code divergence detection, and governed evolution vs. static enforcement — are already captured by existing principles (capture-the-why-not-the-what, dependency-tracking, living-over-stale).
