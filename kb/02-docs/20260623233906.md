## Statement

What makes a *good* `belongs_to` placement is defined **once**, as a single shared definition, and consumed verbatim by every actor that places or re-places a doc in the hierarchy — both the writer that creates docs and the gardener that later refines their homes. A placement definition that is copied into each actor drifts; one authoritative definition cannot.

## What a good placement is

- **Prefer the nearest coherent signpost over the root.** A doc belongs under the most specific grouping that still genuinely contains it, not dumped at the top because the top is always a safe parent.
- **A placement is a navigational claim, not a tag.** `belongs_to` asserts "this doc is part of, and would be orphaned without, that grouping" — it is the membership/hierarchy axis, distinct from the cross-cutting `domain` tags and from the logical `requires` web.
- **One primary parent.** A doc declares the single grouping that defines it, not a scattering of weak memberships.
- **"In the tree somewhere" is necessary but not sufficient.** Being placed at all is not the bar; it must be the *right* branch and a *right-sized* one — a branch that is still navigable, not an overgrown catch-all.
- **When no good parent is visible, defer rather than force one.** A bad placement is worse than a deferred one; leave it for the gardener with full-store context rather than wiring a misleading membership.

## Why one source of truth

This is the boilerplate-include case the skills-compose principle reserves for shared **text** (as opposed to a shared *procedure*, which becomes a sub-skill): the definition is prose, referenced verbatim wherever placement happens. Because the writer that originates a placement and the gardener that refines it read the *same* definition, they cannot diverge on what "good placement" means — the originate step and the refine step stay coherent by construction.
