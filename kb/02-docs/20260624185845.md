## Statement

The set of `domain` tags in use across a store is a **finite shared resource**. Its health is judged qualitatively, not by volume: there is no target count and no per-system rule for how many domains there *should* be — that depends entirely on the size and complexity of the system being documented. The single governing test is that **each domain must justify its own existence** — it must hold water and pull its weight as a distinct grouping a reader would actually filter by.

## What justifies a domain

A domain earns its place by being a coherent, distinct business/problem concern that a reader would want to filter the store by. **A scope-count is not the test.** The earlier heuristic — "a domain typically spans ≥2 scopes" — is rejected as the trigger for whether a domain should exist; scope-spanning may happen, but it neither qualifies nor disqualifies a domain. A domain that does not pull its own weight (it duplicates another, it applies to almost nothing, or it merely restates a scope) does not justify itself.

## Consequences for the shared resource

- **Consolidate what doesn't pull its weight.** Two near-synonym domains (e.g. `acct_management` and `Account Management`) mean *neither* is carrying its own weight; they collapse to one. A domain applied to a single doc, or indistinguishable from another, is a consolidation candidate.
- **Convention consistency over convention choice.** Because the vocabulary is shared, naming conventions matter — but the system is **not opinionated about which** convention is used. Whatever convention a store adopts (casing, separators, singular vs plural) should be applied *consistently*; the discipline is internal consistency, not a prescribed style.
- **An un-pruned vocabulary is the failure mode this discipline exists to prevent — not a liberally-tagged doc.** Noisy, over-applied domains make the facet useless. But judging whether a domain pulls its weight requires *full-store context*, which a writer tagging one doc in isolation does not have. So the discipline is **asymmetric**: enforcement lives at gardening (broad context, prunes and consolidates eagerly), not at origination (isolated context, tags liberally). The bias is not "tag less everywhere" — it is "originate liberally, prune authoritatively" (see [[20260624185902]]).
- **Curation is gardening's job.** Keeping the vocabulary justified — consolidating outliers and synonyms, assigning docs to existing domains, validating that each claimed membership is accurate, and normalizing toward consistent convention — is ongoing maintenance, not a one-time act. It is gardening, with the whole vocabulary in view, that has the standing to prune; the isolated author does not.
