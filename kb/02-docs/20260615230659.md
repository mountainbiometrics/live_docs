## Context

Ids are opaque and titles are descriptive but long, so neither is a friendly handle for humans or agents referencing docs in commands and skill steps. A paragraph-length overview of a doc is also needed in many places (reviews, CLI output, navigation) and must be available as structured data.

## Decision

Every doc carries **three human-facing descriptors**, with a clear primary:

- **`label`** — **required**. A short (2–5 word) Title-Case identifier that *names what the doc is about*; the primary human/agent handle, friendlier than an opaque id. It is never auto-derived — an omitted label is an error.
- **`title`** — **optional**. A sentence-length descriptive elaboration of the label. When omitted it **defaults to the label**, so it is only worth providing when it adds information the label does not.
- **`summary`** — a tight 1–3 sentence overview, held in **frontmatter**.

### Label is required, not derived

A label is non-empty, trimmed, unique across the store (case-insensitive), and matches `^[A-Za-z0-9]+([ -][A-Za-z0-9]+)*$` — letters and digits with single spaces or hyphens between tokens. Whitespace IS allowed, so `label-and-title` and `porcelain api` are both valid; kebab-case remains a special case. Resolution accepts id, exact label, exact title, or a unique case-insensitive substring of title or label — whichever the caller finds most natural. Multi-word labels are quoted on the CLI.

`ldoc new` (and `KB.new`) **require** a non-empty `--label`. There is **no** truncate-the-title fallback: mechanically slicing a title into a label produces meaningless fragments (`State Policy Is`, `The Domain Vocabulary Is`) — the very lazy labels this rule exists to prevent. The author always supplies a meaningful handle that names the subject as a noun phrase, not the opening words of a sentence. On collision with an existing label, the author chooses a distinct one.

#### Label casing convention

A label is a short, human-readable **Title-Case** name — whitespace is allowed and words are capitalised. Examples: `Porcelain API`, `Batch Operations`, `Label and Title`. Kebab slugs (e.g. `porcelain-api`) are valid syntax but are the exceptional case, not the norm. Hand-written labels follow Title Case.

### Title is optional and elaborates the label

When `--title` is omitted it **falls back to the label** verbatim — an absent title costs nothing. Provide a title only when the fuller statement carries information beyond the handle (e.g. label `Singular Ownership`, title `Singular ownership is the ownership face of single responsibility`). A provided title is a complete, informative statement, not a fragment.

### Summary

The `summary` is a **real frontmatter field**: a tight 1–3 sentence overview. It is the single source for overview snapshots used in review output, `ldoc` display, search/navigation blurbs, and future MOC maps. It must not be tied to a body-section parsing rule.

## Consequences

- Humans and agents reference docs by label in `ldoc` commands and prose.
- **Labels are meaningful by construction** — they are author-supplied noun phrases, never truncated from a title, so the lazy-label failure mode is closed at the source.
- Titles stay descriptive without needing to be terse, and an absent title costs nothing because it falls back to the label.
- Display labels stay compact and human-readable (the label is scannable; the title is readable in full context).
- The summary is available as structured data to every tool and skill, decoupled from body formatting.
