## Context

The idea_dump proposed two tag dimensions: `scope` and `domain`. `scope` is now the **topological-zone facet**: a doc's effective scope is the union of `scope` anchors along its `belongs_to` genealogy. `domain` is the other axis and needed its own definition — what it groups, its vocabulary, and its relationship to scope.

## Decision

`domain` groups a doc by the **business or problem domain** it belongs to, independent of which repo or service it pertains to **and independent of scope**. Examples: `Account Management`, `Ontology Mapping`, `Confidence Reporting`. The vocabulary is **open** (not an enum) and **emerges from use** — a store ships with zero domains and does not presume them; the documented subject determines the vocabulary as docs are tagged (see [[20260624185902]]). An empty list (`domain: []`) means the doc applies system-wide or no domain is named.

`domain` and `scope` are **orthogonal in purpose**: `scope` is **topological** (derived from `belongs_to` lineage — *where a doc sits*), whereas `domain` is a **cross-cutting tag** (*what business concern a doc is about*), set directly on the doc and not inherited. Because they answer different questions, their *values* may legitimately **overlap** — most docs in the `viewer` scope might share a `ui` domain — and that correlation is expected and fine, not redundant noise. A doc deep in the `viewer` scope can equally carry `domain: [Ontology Mapping]`.

## Governance: justify-its-existence, not a scope count

`domain` is a **governed** facet, but the governance is qualitative. A domain earns its place only by **justifying its own existence** — pulling its weight as a distinct grouping a reader would filter by (see the finite-shared-resource principle [[20260624185845]]). The earlier heuristic that a domain "typically spans ≥2 scopes" is **not** the test and is no longer applied. The vocabulary is a finite shared resource: gardening consolidates synonyms and outliers and normalizes toward consistent convention.

## domain vs. keywords

`domain` is not the only flat, non-inherited, non-cascade tag field. `keywords` is a *separate* such field, and the two **must not overlap**: a term that is or matches an existing domain belongs as a **domain** tag, not a keyword (see [[20260623233935]]). `domain` is the **governed, filterable grouping facet**; `keywords` is an **ungoverned findability bag** with no justify-its-existence bar. Domain answers "what concern is this about"; keywords answers "what might someone type to find it."

## Consequences

Domain tags enable filtering and indexing by business concern across scope boundaries. They are optional and **advisory** — they do not drive cascade checks. The open vocabulary carries no upfront enum or governance overhead; what governance exists is the ongoing qualitative curation (justify-its-existence, synonym consolidation, convention consistency) performed by gardening.
