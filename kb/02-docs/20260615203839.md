## Context

The idea_dump proposed two tag dimensions: `scope` and `domain`. `scope` is now the **topological-zone facet**: a doc's effective scope is the union of `scope` anchors along its `belongs_to` genealogy. `domain` is the other axis and needed its own definition — what it groups, its vocabulary, and its relationship to scope.

## Decision

`domain` groups a doc by the **business or problem domain** it belongs to, independent of which repo or service it pertains to **and independent of scope**. Examples: `Account Management`, `Ontology Mapping`, `Confidence Reporting`. The vocabulary is open (not an enum) and grows as domains are identified. An empty list (`domain: []`) means the doc applies system-wide or the domain is not yet named.

`domain` and `scope` are **orthogonal axes**: `scope` is **topological** (derived from `belongs_to` lineage — *where a doc sits*), whereas `domain` is a **cross-cutting tag** (*what business concern a doc is about*), set directly on the doc and not inherited through the hierarchy. A doc deep in the `viewer` scope can still carry `domain: [Ontology Mapping]`.

## domain vs. keywords

`domain` is not the only flat, non-inherited, non-cascade tag field. `keywords` is a *separate* such field, and the two must not be conflated. `domain` is a **business-domain grouping facet** — it carries a governance bar (a tag worth applying typically spans ≥2 scopes, and a gardening pass normalizes the vocabulary to prevent synonym drift). `keywords` is a **findability field** — a free-text bag of search synonyms/aliases with **no** governance bar, existing only so a searcher's term surfaces the doc. Domain answers "what concern is this about"; keywords answers "what might someone type to find it."

## Consequences

Domain tags enable filtering and indexing by business concern across scope boundaries. The open vocabulary means no governance overhead beyond periodic gardening normalization (e.g. `Account Mgmt` vs `Account Management`). Domain tags are optional and advisory — they do not drive cascade checks.
