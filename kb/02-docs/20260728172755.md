## Statement

Documents couple to each other through **stable abstractions** — the durable claims they make — not through another document's implementation details.

An edge is healthy when it would still make sense after the target's how-details were rewritten. If rewriting an incidental algorithm, file path, schema field, or procedural step in B forces A to change, A was coupled to B's implementation, not to B's claim.

## Why

Loose coupling is a coding principle applied to documentation ([[20260625222626]]). Modules that depend on each other's interfaces rather than internals stay editable; the same test applies to docs.

High cohesion ([[20260728173103]]) keeps a doc's *internal* parts related to one concept. Loose coupling governs the *edges between* docs. Without a coupling discipline, even cohesive atomic docs glue themselves to each other's churn: every surgical edit fans out because neighbors bound themselves to transient detail rather than to the stable claim.

Depend on the *interface* of a doc — the principle, constraint, requirement, or goal it asserts — and leave its how-details free to change. Explicit dependency tracking ([[20260615100002]]) makes those couplings checkable; this principle says which couplings are worth writing.
