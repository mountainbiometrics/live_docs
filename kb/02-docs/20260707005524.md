## Statement

live_docs holds **intent-bearing knowledge** — the *why* behind a system: its decisions, principles, constraints, goals, and the reasoning that produced them. Its job is to keep that knowledge **accurate** — true and internally consistent — as reality changes. That is a **write-time** job.

## Rationale

Keeping the store true is what the system is *for*; retrieving from it is a separate concern. Retrieval — getting the right doc in front of a reader or an agent — happens at read time, *over* a store that is already true; it does nothing to keep it true. And retrieval is the easy, well-understood half: an index over a corpus is a solved shape. The hard, unsolved half is whether the corpus is *worth* retrieving — up to date, non-contradictory, non-redundant. Fetching from a stale or conflicting store just makes a reader confidently wrong faster.

So the effort goes into keeping intent-bearing knowledge true — a write-time discipline of atomicity, explicit dependencies, and cascade — not into the pipe that serves it. Get the store right first; any read-time layer bolts onto it cleanly. This is the whole-store counterpart to living-over-stale ([[20260615100001]]): that principle judges a single doc, this one names what the whole store is *for*.
