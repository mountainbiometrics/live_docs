## Statement

Enforcing conformance to a decision is only half the job, and the easy half. The hard half is **evolving** the decision as reality changes — and a decision can be safely revised only when the two things that justify it are recorded: its **why** (the rationale, and the alternatives it beat) and its **dependencies** (what rests on it). A rule carrying neither is enforceable but not safely revisable.

## Rationale

A rulebook that can be checked but not safely changed fails in one of two ways: it **ossifies** — enforced long after it stopped making sense — or it is **quietly ignored**, because people route around a rule they cannot justify. Both are drift.

Recording the why makes a decision *challengeable on its merits*: a reader can tell whether it still applies and what would have to be true to overturn it ([[20260625222613]]). Recording the dependencies makes the *blast radius* of changing it computable — everything that must move with it is visible ([[20260615100002]]), and cascade ([[20260615100012]]) carries the revision through. Together they turn a decision from a static assertion into a node in a living graph that can be revised in place. Enforcement — "does this change conflict with a prior decision?" — is one affordance of that graph, not the whole of it. The harder, primary job is keeping the decisions themselves current and mutually consistent as reality moves.
