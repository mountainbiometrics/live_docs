## Decision

During an open session, every mutation appends **one WAL line** to the session's record — the WAL is the **single in-flight log** of what changed. The per-doc **`history:`** is *not* written per mutation. At **close**, the WAL is collapsed into **one history entry per touched doc** (its dominant change_type, best author note, and last-touch time) written to the docs, and the session's **review** is built from the same WAL. History is a *projection* of the WAL, so the two can never diverge.

## Why the WAL is the in-flight source of truth

Writing a per-doc history entry for every keystroke bloats a doc's trail — one session that creates and then refines a doc five times would leave five entries where one belongs. Keeping the granular log in the WAL and projecting it to a single session-granular history entry at close keeps each doc's trail readable (one entry per session that touched it) while the full detail survives in the review's WAL archive. A **deletion** also needs the WAL: it removes the doc, so it leaves no history — the WAL line records the dead doc's label/type and the edges stripped, so the change stays auditable and **replayable**.

## Entry shape

Each WAL line records the operation, the target ref, the **change_type(s)** it touched (a list — see the change-type taxonomy), and the note (flagged author-supplied or auto). The collapsed history entry carries the single **dominant** change_type. The timestamp-window (`--since`) path is retained as a fallback for building a review without a session.
