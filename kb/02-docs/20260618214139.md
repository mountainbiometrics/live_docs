## Statement

Deciding which docs form a coherent group and writing an aggregated overview of that group are acts of **judgment performed by skills**, not deterministic rollups produced by tooling. A mechanical concatenation of child summaries is not a map.

## Rationale

A map is synthesized at a **higher altitude** than any single member: it collapses overlap and surfaces the connective tissue the members do not state about each other. The value of a signpost is reader cognitive-load reduction, and that requires editorial synthesis, not aggregation. Tooling can list members deterministically (a doc's descendants are read off `belongs_to`), but it cannot decide what the grouping *means* or write the overview that makes the subtree graspable without opening every child.

## Relationship

This is the navigation/curation expression of the mutators-dumb / skills-judge split: `ldoc` and the scripts behind it stay dumb, while the grouping and summarizing judgment lives in skills. That judgment is exercised by the gardening family's structural phases — **garden-hierarchy** (deciding which docs form a group and wiring membership) and **garden-summarize** (synthesizing the group's overview). The signpost role is **structural** — it belongs to any descendant-bearing doc, not to a special `index` type — but *which* docs should form a group and *what the group means* remain agentic judgments.
