## Use-case

Docs in the store are intentionally tiny and single-responsibility. The reading consequence is that a reader constantly needs *a little more* than the doc in front of them — and forcing a full navigation (losing place, context, scroll) for each peek would make the atomic store painful to read.

So the viewer's stance is: **navigation should be rare**. The reader pulls in what they need through **four escalating inlining tiers**, only the last of which actually moves them:

1. **Hover** — a tooltip showing the referenced doc's summary. The cheapest peek.
2. **Single-click** — expand the referenced doc's body **in place**, without leaving the current view.
3. **Index composition** — inline a whole grouping: an index's guide plus its members, recombined as sections.
4. **Double-click** — **navigate / refocus** on the referenced doc. The only tier that changes where the reader is.

## Rationale

Matching escalation to intent means most reading needs are met in-place; the reader escalates to true navigation only when they genuinely want to relocate. This is what makes a heavily-decomposed store comfortable to read.
