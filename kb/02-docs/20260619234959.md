## Context

The type taxonomy carried an `index` value for docs that act as navigational signposts over a grouping. Being a signpost is not a kind of thing a doc is. It is a structural position a doc occupies. A component, a requirement, a principle, or a decision can all sit above a subtree and orient a reader to it.

## Decision

`index` is removed as a doc type. A doc earns the signpost role structurally: by having descendants, incoming `belongs_to` edges. Any type can have descendants and thus play that role. The role is read off the topology. It is not declared by a type value.

A heading ([[20261007193653]]) is the type for a parent that asserts nothing but that grouping. It is one kind of signpost ([[20261007193652]]). A decision, a requirement, or a component with children remains a signpost of its own type.

The former `index` docs are re-typed to whatever they actually are: the root and the structural subsystems become components (each anchoring a scope facet), and Foundational Principles becomes a requirement. Their orientation-guide bodies and summaries are unchanged. Those belong to any descendant-bearing doc, not only to a heading.

## Consequences

- The type taxonomy denotes what a doc asserts. A heading asserts that it only groups its children. It does not assert the signpost position, which any parent holds.
- Skills that grouped under `type:index` group under any descendant-bearing doc.
- The viewer finds entry points and builds its hierarchy from topology, not from a type.
