## Context

Two kinds of relationships between docs emerged during implementation: structural dependencies (doc A's reasoning or correctness relies on doc B) and provenance links (doc A was informed by, or grew from, doc B). Both were initially recorded as `depends_on`, causing cascade-check to treat provenance links as structural edges — triggering unnecessary walks and inflating the dependency graph.

The single-directional depends_on decision (20260615100007) established that only forward `depends_on` is stored, with reverse generated. But it did not distinguish provenance from structural dependency.

## Decision

`depends_on` records structural dependency: a change to the upstream doc may invalidate or require updating the downstream doc. These are cascade edges; cascade-check walks them.

`references` records provenance: the downstream doc was informed by, grew from, or cites the upstream doc for context, but does not structurally depend on it. References are NOT cascade edges. Adding or removing a `references` link is a references-only change: no history entry is required and cascade-check is not run.

## Consequences

- Provenance migrations (moving NORM_ID from depends_on to references across many docs) can be applied in bulk with no per-doc cascade overhead.
- The dependency graph becomes structurally accurate: only genuine structural dependencies drive cascade walks.
- Reference docs (type: reference) are the natural upstream of `references` links; decision and principle docs are the natural upstream of `depends_on` links.
- The revise-doc skill's "references-only" exception is grounded in this distinction.

## Correction

The field names `depends_on` and `references` are superseded by a typed edge vocabulary in which each relationship type carries its own cascade behavior, structural semantics, and naming. See [[20260617144634]] (edge type vocabulary) and [[20260617144637]] (provenance replaces references).
