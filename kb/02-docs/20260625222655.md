## What this is

A guide to choosing — and not over-using — the `decision` type. The operative
classification ladder (all the types, the "is this really a decision?" test)
lives in `.claude/skills/_shared/doc-types.md`; this records *why* the decision
type is drawn the way it is.

## Why decisions are special

Almost any fact about the system was, at some level, decided — so `decision` is
the type most easily over-applied, and in this store it became the largest and
most misclassified type. Reserving it for **architectural choices among
alternatives** is what keeps the taxonomy meaningful: if everything is a
decision, the type tells a reader nothing.

## What qualifies

A `decision` records a deliberate choice among real alternatives, with a
rationale, that future work shouldn't have to re-decide. Two consequences:

- **Architectural, not existential.** It must be restatable as "X over Y because
  Z," and **excisable** — swapping the choice (a serialization format, a
  library) should be a one-atomic-doc change, the doc analogue of good
  architecture. A "decision" that merely asserts a thing *exists* is a
  `component`; one that tells an actor *how to work* is a `guide`.
- **Scoped to the level it binds.** Place it via `belongs_to` under the subtree
  it governs, so it constrains that subtree and not its siblings. A decision with
  **no** `belongs_to` is **global** — legitimate for genuinely cross-cutting
  architecture (the foundational principles are effectively global), wrong for
  one that should bind only a single project. (See the placement shared
  definition for what the hierarchy means.)
