## Context

`ldoc find` matches a doc by its title, label, and body, and filters by `scope`/`domain`. A searcher who reaches for a synonym or an alias the author did not happen to use in those fields cannot surface the doc. `domain` does not fill this gap: it is a governed business-grouping facet, not a bag of search terms.

## Decision

`keywords` is a distinct frontmatter field: a **flat list of search synonyms and aliases** that exists purely for *findability*. Like `domain`, it is flat — **not inherited** through the hierarchy and **not a cascade edge**; it is optional, and an omitted field means the empty list. Unlike `domain`, it carries **no governance bar** — keywords are free-text terms a searcher might plausibly type, added freely with no vocabulary discipline and no "is this a real domain" test.

`keywords` and `domain` are therefore **separate axes**: `domain` groups a doc by the business/problem concern it is about (the curated, ≥2-scopes grouping facet); `keywords` makes a doc *findable* under the terms a reader would search for. A doc can carry either, both, or neither.

## Consequences

- `ldoc new` and `ldoc set` accept `--keywords` (comma-separated, replace semantics — the same shape as `--domain`).
- `ldoc find` includes `keywords` in its term match alongside title/label/body, with an optional `--keyword` filter.
- `validate` treats keywords as free-text: trimmed and de-duped per doc, with no reference resolution — there is nothing for a keyword to point at.
- Because there is no governance bar, curating keywords is surface-quality work, not a structural decision.
