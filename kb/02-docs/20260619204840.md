## Context

Single Responsibility pushes docs to be tiny and decomposed on disk. Readers, however, often want a colocated whole — the full picture of a subtree in one place. That is a real tension, and the wrong fix would be to colocate on disk (which would violate decomposition).

## Decision

Resolve the tension at the **presentation layer** using **descendant-bearing docs** as the **recombination mechanism**. Rendering such a doc is:

- its **orientation-guide body** (the synthesis a reader reads to get oriented), plus
- on demand, its **member docs' bodies inlined as sections**, gathered by following `belongs_to`.

Docs stay **decomposed and single-responsibility on disk**; the viewer **recombines** them into a readable whole only at view time, via the same `![[id]]` transclusion path.

## Rationale

This keeps the disk store faithful to decomposition while giving readers the colocated reading experience they want, without duplicating content. The recombining doc already exists as the navigational signpost — by virtue of having descendants, not by a special `index` type — and carries an orientation guide; making it the recombination surface is a natural extension. The guide frames the members, and the members fill in the detail on demand.
