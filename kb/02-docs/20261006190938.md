## Decision

Each doc's `intent` records what the person did to make its claim exist:

| Value | The person… |
|---|---|
| `requested` | came forward with this request specifically |
| `chosen` | chose this specific option when presented with a choice or decision |
| `incidental` | let it happen: an agent's proposal, a provisional adoption, anything merely committed or signed off |

`intent` replaces `level`. Every doc except a reference carries it, and a new doc starts at `incidental`. Committing or signing off a doc does not raise its intent. A doc still carrying `level` gets a validate warning that names the migration.

`ldoc ls --intent incidental` lists the claims the person has not requested or chosen: the queue of docs awaiting their ratification.

Three rungs, over a longer ladder with trial, proposed, or ratified steps, because these are the three buckets the person distinguishes.

## Why

The store has to record whether a claim was asked for or let happen ([[20261006190930]]). `level` was meant to carry that, but it was never defined well enough to use: it was unclear when to use it, how, or what each value meant, or when a doc should be distinguished by `status` rather than `level`. Its name also suggested a number on a scale. A person who commits a doc has in a sense approved it, but that does not make the claim any less incidental.
