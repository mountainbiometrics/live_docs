## Decision

`imposed_by` records what makes a claim hold:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `environment` | the world: regulation, a vendor, physics, a customer |
| `tradeoff` | it follows from a choice recorded elsewhere in the store |
| `choice` | the team set it directly |

It is optional on every claim type, required on constraints, and forbidden on headings and references. A heading asserts a grouping, not a claim that holds for a reason ([[20261007193653]]). Constraints take any of the three values: `tradeoff` for a technical constraint, `choice` for an organizational constraint such as a standard the team set, and `environment` for an external one. It appears in `get` and `show`.

The value decides the claim's upstream edge:

- a `tradeoff` claim must `require` the decision or component it follows from;
- a `choice` claim should `require` the goal, use-case, or principle that motivates it;
- an `environment` claim needs no upstream edge, only provenance pointing at the source of the force.

## Why

A claim can say whether it was chosen or given, and a technical constraint that follows from an accepted trade-off differs from an organizational one set by choice ([[20261006190933]]). A constraint admits `choice` because an organizational constraint is set by the team, so it has no other valid value. Requiring the upstream decision of a `tradeoff` claim means that when the decision changes, cascade reaches the claims that followed from it.
