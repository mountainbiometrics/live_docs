## Context

The edge vocabulary already separates `belongs_to` (hierarchy) from `requires` (logical dependency), but the *consequence* of choosing one over the other was left vague. With scope now derived from topology, the distinction becomes load-bearing and must be stated explicitly.

## Decision

`belongs_to` and `requires` are distinguished by **what they do to scope and lineage**:

- **`belongs_to` is the family-tree edge.** It forms a hierarchical DAG that **defines scope**. By belonging to a parent, a doc is **scoped under that parent and its entire genealogy** — it inherits every `scope` facet along the chain. Use `belongs_to` when a doc should live inside a zone and take on its lineage.

- **`requires` is dependency without membership.** It expresses that a doc **needs** another, but **does not** pull the depender into the dependency's family tree and does **not** make it inherit the dependency's scope or lineage. Use `requires` when a doc depends on another but must stay outside its zone.

**Effective scope is computed over `belongs_to` only — never `requires`.** A doc can `require` something in a different zone without being absorbed into that zone; only its `belongs_to` ancestry determines its facets.

## Consequences

- Choosing `belongs_to` vs `requires` is now a **scoping decision**, not just a cascade-flavor decision: it determines which zone a doc belongs to.
- This wires directly into the topological-scope model: the genealogy walked for effective scope is the `belongs_to` chain, so a `requires` edge to a doc in another zone is invisible to scope.
- Cascade behavior is unchanged (both are hard cascade edges); what is newly explicit is that only one of them confers scope/lineage.
