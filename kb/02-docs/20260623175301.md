## Decision

Because shards may **overlap** (the same passage can land in more than one shard), the same concept can be extracted twice when two children are ingested. shard-clipping does **not** try to prevent this at split time. Instead, **duplicates are reconciled downstream**:

- **map-concepts-to-docs** runs a concept-matching pass during each child's ingest, so a concept already recorded by an earlier shard is matched to the existing doc rather than duplicated.
- The **gardening passes** (single-responsibility, consistency, dedup) catch any duplication that slips through and merge or relink it.

This reconciliation machinery is owned by the ingest/apply workflows (`[[20260618000147]]` apply-to-docs runs the same map-concepts + cascade discipline).

## Consequence: shard ingests need no ordering

Because duplicates are tolerated and reconciled after the fact, the children of a sharded clipping can be **ingested in any order** — no serialization, no "ingest shard A before shard B" constraint. This is what lets pending child raws drain through the backlog loop independently. The decision builds on full-coverage-with-overlap (`[[20260623175240]]`): it is precisely *because* overlap is allowed that downstream reconciliation is required, and *because* reconciliation is downstream that ordering is free.
