## Constraint

The pipeline must preserve **three timestamps with three distinct meanings**, and must not conflate or silently drop any of them:

- **`authored_at`** — when the **source material** was written (may be fuzzy).
- **`captured`** — when the material was **dropped into the inbox** (gate 0).
- **`imported`** — when the material was **promoted to the raw tier** (gate 1).

A reader reconstructing an item's lineage needs all three: how old the *content* is, how long it sat *uncaptured* before someone preserved it, and how long it sat in the *inbox* before being accepted. Collapsing them loses provenance.

## Why this is a constraint, not a convenience

The previous pipeline violated this. Only `imported` survived to the raw tier — **`captured` was silently dropped at promotion**, and there was **nowhere to record `authored_at` at all**. So an item's recorded age conflated "when we promoted it" with "how old the content is," which is exactly the conflation that breaks staleness reasoning across heterogeneous sources. Mixing a two-year-old doc with a two-week-old one is only legible if `authored_at` is preserved independently of `captured` and `imported`.

This generalizes the reference-doc distinction (imported-timestamp), which already separated `imported` from `created`/source-age for `reference` docs, to the **raw tier and the whole capture path** — and adds the missing `captured`/`authored_at` axes.
