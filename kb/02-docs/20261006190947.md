## Decision

`status` records a doc's lifecycle and nothing else:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `living` | the claim is current |
| `deprecated` | the claim was overturned; `superseded_by` points at its replacement |
| `reference` | frozen source material, never a truth claim |

`target` is retired, and what it carried moves elsewhere:

- An intended claim that is not built yet is `living` with `realization: planned`, or `realization: deferred` when it is deliberately put off ([[20261006190943]]).
- When a current path and a desired path coexist, the current doc stays `living` and carries `superseded_by` pointing at its planned successor. Validate requires `superseded_by` on a deprecated doc and allows it on a living one ([[20260617212602]]).

A doc still carrying `status: target` gets a validate warning that names the migration.

## Why

With `status` and `level` side by side, nobody could say when a doc should be distinguished by one rather than the other, and "is it in code" and "is it in the present reality" sounded like the same question. With realization in its own field, `status` answers one question: is this claim current?
