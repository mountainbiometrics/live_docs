## Context

The schema had two orthogonal fields: `state: actual|target` (temporal intent — is this realized?) and `status: living|historical` (lifecycle — is this still current?). In practice these were almost never orthogonal: a doc was either current-and-actual, intended-but-not-yet-real, or obsolete. The extra field added redundancy without expressiveness.

## Decision

The `state` field is removed. `status` becomes a four-value enum:

| Value | Meaning | Old equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| `living` | Current active truth; maintained to reflect reality | status:living + state:actual |
| `target` | Intended design goal; not yet realized; still maintained | state:target |
| `deprecated` | Was true; now false; superseded | status:historical |
| `reference` | Frozen supporting material (clippings, brainstorms, external sources) | type:reference |

The axis these values encode is 'what is this doc's relationship to present reality' — a single axis, not two.

## Consequences

- Every `type: reference` doc is `status: reference` — frozen, not a truth claim.
- The old `state: target` docs are now `status: target` — they remain living for cascade purposes (see is_living decision).
- The old `status: historical` docs become `status: deprecated` and require a superseded_by edge plus a Correction section.
- Tooling and validate enforce the new enum; the `state` field is rejected as unknown.

## Correction

`target` is retired, and `status` now records lifecycle only: `living`, `deprecated`, or `reference` ([[20261006190947]]). The "relationship to present reality" this enum encoded is two questions, answered by two fields: whether a claim is current (`status`), and whether the thing it claims exists in the implementation (`realization`). A deferred claim is `living` with `realization: deferred`; a current path that coexists with its planned replacement stays `living` and carries `superseded_by`.
