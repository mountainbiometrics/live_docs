Redesign of the domain tag model. A domain's existence is justified qualitatively (it must pull its own weight as a distinct, filterable grouping), not by a scope-count test — the prior '≥2 scopes' heuristic is rejected as the trigger. The domain vocabulary is a finite shared resource curated qualitatively rather than by volume targets, since the right number of domains depends entirely on the documented system's complexity. live_docs is unopinionated about naming convention but encourages consistent use of whatever convention is adopted. Domains emerge from use (the system ships with zero and does not presume a user's domains); they are identified at tag time, where a new ldoc registry capability lists in-use domains and nudges reuse over invention. Gardening curates the shared vocabulary (consolidate synonyms/outliers, assign docs to existing domains, validate claimed memberships, enforce convention-consistency) rather than applying a scope-count test. Scope/domain value overlap is expected and acceptable; keywords and domains must not overlap (a term matching a domain belongs as a domain). Over-eager tagging is the failure mode all governance exists to prevent; under-tagging is the correct bias.
