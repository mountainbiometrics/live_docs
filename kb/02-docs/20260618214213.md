## Statement

The store must be explorable through a **dedicated reader**. To make exploration useful:

- nodes and links read as `{type}: {label}` — never opaque ids,
- the distinct edge types are visually distinguishable,
- a doc's summary is reachable on hover.

## Rationale

The store is intentionally **atomic and id-opaque**, which makes flat directory browsing hard. A typed, labeled, navigable reading surface restores human navigability — a reader can see clusters, follow edge types, and read summaries without opening files.

## How the goal is met

This navigability goal is now met by the **purpose-built, read-only HTML viewer**, which resolves every reference to its label with a summary tooltip in a single render pass and offers hierarchy, facet, and (deferred) graph navigation.

**Obsidian** was explored as the reader — config-only wins applied directly, richer needs met by read-only plugins, with write-back excluded by the one-schema-authority constraint. It has been **set aside** as the chosen reader because it leaks opaque ids and resolves wiki-links inconsistently across surfaces; the underlying navigability goal recorded here is unchanged and reader-agnostic.
