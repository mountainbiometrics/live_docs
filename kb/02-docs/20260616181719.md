## Decision

Review challenges a change **after the fact**; it does **not** gate the change. A change is considered *blessed on creation* whether it has been reviewed or not. The review layer exists to **contest and correct** already-actualized changes, not to approve them before they take effect.

## Rationale

Gating review would reintroduce the drift this system fights: changes would queue behind an approval step, the live store would lag reality, and "Living over Stale" would be violated while material sat unblessed. Making changes blessed-on-creation keeps the store always-live; the review layer adds a correction loop on top rather than a checkpoint in front. This mirrors the git-optional audit stance: provenance and accountability are layered *after* the change is recorded, not required *before* it.

## Reconciling the tension with cascade

This does **not** contradict cascade's HALT verdicts. Cascade-check can emit `incompatible` or `context-request` and stop mid-cascade (see cascade-verdicts). That halt is a **different layer**: it is *consistency enforcement* — the system detecting a contradiction or a gap it cannot auto-resolve and demanding human attention to keep the graph coherent. It is not pre-emptive *quality review* of intent.

The two coexist:
- **Cascade HALT** fires during propagation when a change makes a neighbor logically `incompatible` (or the judge lacks context). It guards graph consistency. It can interrupt actualization because an inconsistent store is incoherent, not merely unreviewed.
- **Post-hoc review** fires after a coherent change has landed. It contests *intent/judgment* ("was this the right change?"), not *consistency* ("does this contradict its neighbors?").

A change can therefore be blessed-on-creation (no review gate) yet still be HALTed by cascade if it breaks consistency. Review never gates; cascade halts only for unresolved inconsistency.
