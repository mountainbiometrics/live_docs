## Statement

When documents are created by agentic processes — where independent subagents handle different parts of the same pipeline — the output will exhibit **intermittent, non-deterministic inconsistency** in naming, formatting, and structure. This is a structural property of multi-agent pipelines, not an edge case or occasional bug.

Examples of the pattern: labels that appear truncated ('ddl entities are', 'State Policy is'); docs with elaborate section headings while neighboring docs are bare paragraphs; summaries that are fragments or verbatim copies of the first body line.

## Why full-scan validation does not catch this

Schema validation (structural integrity checks) detects missing required fields, broken refs, and constraint violations — it is deterministic and exhaustive. Editorial inconsistency — lazy naming, malformed summaries, shape mismatches — is neither a schema violation nor consistently present. Running a full scan for editorial quality is both expensive and unlikely to find the right things.

## The correct QA response

**Sampling-based spot-checking** is the appropriate quality assurance strategy for sporadic agentic inconsistency. A representative sample catches the tail of the inconsistency distribution without requiring full coverage, and can be tuned over time as inconsistency patterns become clearer. The sample size and selection policy are configuration knobs, not fixed values. Manual review is not the alternative — the goal is an automated, intermittent sweep that applies editorial judgment at sampling depth.
