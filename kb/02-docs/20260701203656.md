## Component

An **editing session** is the concrete unit of editing work in the store — the **actualized** form of what used to be only a virtual notion, the "episode." Where an episode was merely the implied span between a start time and a review, a session is a real, **id-identified, on-disk** thing that an owner opens and closes.

## Lifecycle

1. **Open** (`session start`) — one owner opens a session and receives its id. Mutations require an open session and auto-create one if absent (see sessions-are-required).
2. **Stamp** — every change during the session **double-writes**: a per-doc history entry and a line in the session's on-disk change log (the WAL).
3. **Close** (`session close`) — rolls the WAL up into exactly **one** review (see [[20260616181820]]) and deletes the live record. A session maps one-to-one onto a review.

Between open and close a session is **inspectable and resumable**: `session list` shows open sessions with their age and note-coverage; `session summary` sets the session's **agent recap** — the one-line intent of the whole session, the equivalent of a commit message or PR title, authored by the agent doing the work rather than a human afterward; `session resume` re-exports an id to keep working under it.

## Merging fragments

Because the id pointer is ambient and each `ldoc` process is separate, an agent that forgets to export its id produces several one-command sessions. `session merge` folds such fragments into the **earliest** of them — re-tagging their changes and concatenating their WALs in time order — so the work still closes as one review.

## Why it is its own thing

Making the session concrete — rather than leaving the episode implicit in timestamps — is what lets session membership be exact, lets the review be built mechanically from what the session actually touched, and lets independent tools and subagents cooperate on one unit of work.
