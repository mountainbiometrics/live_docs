## Decision

A stored claim informs the next change. It does not restrict it ([[20261006190938]]).

Intent is a chain: `requested` > `chosen` > `incidental`, and a missing intent is `incidental`. Listings show that order ([[20261006190951]]). None of it is a lock.

A change carries an intent too: the person's own words are `requested`, a proposal they went along with is `chosen`, an agent's own judgment is `incidental`. Compare the two on the same claim. At or above the doc's intent, the change updates the doc, assertion included. Below it, the assertion stays and the new claim is written beside it as its own doc, related and named in the report. Neither case refuses or pauses.

"I want abc" and "we should build xyz" are usually two claims, not one. Record both and relate them.

How to convey it: when a clarification changes a plan, add the goal the old plan had left unarticulated so both are visible. When the person's words supersede an earlier request of theirs, say so as context, then write.

## Why

The store is a signpost for what was said, how much stood behind it, and what else it touches. Using an earlier claim as a reason to refuse the next one raises the guardrail the facets were added to lower ([[20261006190926]]). The earlier claim is how the agent knows what just changed. An unguided pass needs no separate rule: its changes are `incidental`, and the comparison already says what that does.
