# Request: replace `level` with intent, force, realization, imposition; make type decide which facets apply; surface facets on every listing

Source: design session 2026-09-28 → 2026-10-06 between the owner (Samuel) and the orchestrating agent. Part A is the owner's reasoning in their own words (verbatim quotes, lightly trimmed). Part B is the agreed design. Part C records rulings the owner made during the session. Everything in B and C traces to a quote in A or to an owner ruling in C; nothing else is owner intent.

---

## A. The owner's words (the whys)

On why this refinement exists:

> "I was sick of having coding agents refusing to make iterative changes because 'Sinai had ratified the shape' … so I started pushing harder and harder for 'no implementation details' … There are parts of that that I think are important, but there's also a silliness there; if people want to record implementation details, then the system should allow that. What would be better is if the 'types' actually started being used for things. Like: if a doc is for a component or an architectural decision, then it's relevant to ask/record/try-to-keep-track-of whether it has actually been implemented or not (compared to a 'principle' which just exists, whether or not any implementation adheres to it)."

> "I wanted to be better at tracking what was 'incidental' vs. what was explicitly asked for, so the agents there started a convention of putting provenance tracking in the body of the doc; specifically so they could differentiate 'this was asked for directly' vs. 'an agent suggested this, and the user didn't refute it'. As much as it should be fine for a store to have their own conventions and writing styles: this purpose should be built in. That's the whole point of the doc status levels."

> "Those levels/statuses aren't well defined and articulated (it's not clear when to use them, or how to use them, or what each one even means (I can't even say when something should be differentiated by `status` vs. `level`…)), and they're mechanically nothing more than a note. They should show up in the results/tooling/renderings; almost as part of the title. Like, a doc labeled: 'Decision #30' looks important and enforceable and like gospel truth; but 'Deprecated: ~Decision #30~' doesn't, nor 'Incidental: Decision #30'. And not that it should be prepended everywhere, but the various outputs give titles, ids, maybe the type, and maybe a summary; but never the 'level' so agents have to fetch and make sense of the entire doc just to learn if it's something it actually needs to take into consideration. We should be much better at surfacing the most intentional docs (definitely un-surface things that are only around for reference's sake) and then surfacing the intention level per doc in lists and stuff."

> "I believe that with these two improvements, I would be much more comfortable letting agents sync up coding work with the design docs and vice versa."

On the intent scale:

> "If a person commits a doc, then they have in essence 'approved it', but that doesn't make it any less incidental."

> "'Level' isn't as good of a term… it sounds like maybe I put the number 5 there or something. But I do want that; I want 'I came forward with this request specifically', 'I chose this specific option when presented a choice/decision', and 'I let this happen' are the three rungs/buckets I want."

> "Having a mechanical check would be great; having that check be 'must carry a `## Basis` section' puts it weirdly in the body text, and that's not a good place. It should be a frontmatter field, and a clearer name."

On force:

> "We're losing the line between 'required' and 'recommended'… I think that there's a super important difference between a rule and a guideline (even if it's often a subtle difference)."

On realization:

> "I like having the 'realization' separated out, but I don't think it's necessarily intuitive how that differs from 'status'. ('is it in code' and 'is it in the present reality' can sound like the exact same thing)."

> "'Realization' is a good field name, cause it can't mean much other than what it is."

On types and the why-chain:

> "I keep feeling like I should get rid of the types, but I guess they do have semantic meaning if nothing else, and that is definitely relevant in this system… I guess I also just keep wanting to do something more mechanically with them."

> "Decisions/components should almost always depend on principles/constraints/requirements, which should in turn almost always depend on goals and use-cases (which are two types that should be expressed _way_ more than they are…)."

> "I was wanting a table of desired properties per type (eg: a decision requires these properties, could have these properties, and should not have these properties)."

> "I'm not sure that I would want a `tier` property (esp. if the type is always of the same tier)."

On imposition (chosen vs given):

> "'Chosen or given', that's a good point, and that almost deserves its own field (maybe?) and could apply to all sorts of things: did we choose this internally (is it a self-assigned rule) vs. forced on us through some external thing (environmental constraint)?"

> "Knowing that something was a technical constraint (ie: it's constrained by the technology that was accepted elsewhere (ie: it was part of an accepted trade-off)) vs. an organizational constraint (ie: it's constrained by a standard/principle that was arbitrarily set)."

> "If it's about 'is this internal/self-imposed vs. external' then 'agency' isn't a very intuitive name… `imposed` or `source` or almost anything would be better."

On published templates (ADR, NFR):

> "I don't want to add, enforce, or even advise on any of that."

On tooling posture and migration:

> "All of our tooling should follow the principles of 'loose on input and strict on output', so if there are fields we expect but don't see, those can be 'warned' about, but everything should keep functioning, and if there are fields we don't expect, it's okay that they exist, we just don't do anything with them."

> "Migration can be done as a script or an agentic process or something downstream, and I'd love to have that set up in a normalized and repeatable fashion, but that's a followup concern."

> "Migrating docs to a new structure shouldn't be limited to using the ldoc cli like other adjustments; it's fine to manually (or en' bulk) apply structural edits to docs."

## B. The design

`level` is removed. `status` loses `target`. Five fields are added. Each answers one question.

### intent — what the person did to make this claim exist
- Values: `requested` (they brought it forward), `chosen` (they picked this option when presented a choice), `incidental` (they let it happen: agent proposals, provisional adoptions, anything merely committed or signed off).
- Required on every doc except references. Default at creation: `incidental`.
- Rendered as the first word of every doc line: "Requested decision: …", "Incidental component: …".
- Ranks within status: requested, then chosen, then incidental.
- Agent permission rule: a `requested` or `chosen` claim is never altered by an agent; the agent records a conflicting `incidental` doc and flags it. An `incidental` claim may be revised freely with a history note.
- Absent (legacy, unassessed): rendered "Unattributed"; treated as `chosen` for the permission rule until assessed.

### intent_basis — the evidence
- String: the quote or citation (session, review, raw clipping, date).
- Required iff intent is `requested` or `chosen`; the mutation that sets those refuses without it.
- Shown in get and show only, never in lists.

### force — how hard the claim binds
- Values: `must` (a rule; deviation is a conflict), `should` (a guideline; deviation allowed with a stated reason), `may` (an allowance).
- Only on normative types: principle, constraint, requirement, decision, guide. Forbidden on component, goal, use-case, reference.
- Cascade-check: a conflict against a `must` is incompatible; against a `should` needs clarification. Agents may deviate from a `should` in code with a note; never from a `must` without the person.
- Trailing tag in listings.

### realization — does the thing the doc claims exist in the implementation
- Values: `realized`, `partial`, `planned`, `deferred`, `unassessed` (migrated or ingested, nobody has checked).
- Only on realizable types: goal ("reached"), use-case ("supported"), requirement ("met"), decision ("in effect"), component ("exists"). Required there, no default. Forbidden on principle, constraint, guide, reference.
- Companions (optional): `realization_refs` (list of anchors: paths, symbols, URLs), `realization_verified` (date or commit).
- `planned` on a living doc is the build backlog. `deferred` never counts as drift.
- Trailing tag in listings.
- A sync skill walks realizable docs in a scope, checks anchors, sets the value and verified stamp, reports planned-but-unbuilt docs and built-but-undocumented things (handed to reconcile-changes).
- Implementation details are allowed: anchors are pointers, and a body may carry an `## Implementation` section that is volatile, exempt from the why-not-what rule, refreshed by sync, never a truth claim.

### imposed_by — what makes this claim hold
- Values: `environment` (the world: regulation, vendor, physics, a customer), `tradeoff` (it follows from a choice recorded elsewhere in the store), `choice` (we set it directly).
- Optional on all claim types; required on constraints (`environment` or `tradeoff`).
- Why-chain mechanics: a `tradeoff` doc must require the decision or component it follows from; a `choice` doc should require the goal, use-case, or principle that motivates it; an `environment` doc needs no upstream edge, only provenance.
- Shown in get and show.

### status — lifecycle only
- Values: `living`, `deprecated`, `reference`.
- `target` is retired. Deferral is `realization: deferred`. The coexisting pair (a current path and a desired path) becomes `superseded_by` on the still-living current doc pointing at its planned successor. Validate: `superseded_by` required when deprecated, allowed when living.

### The types

Required = validate error if absent on a doc the tool writes; on existing docs a missing expected field is a warning (loose on input). Expected = validate warning. Forbidden = validate error if present.

| type | force | realization | imposed_by | expected `requires` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| goal | forbidden | required ("reached") | optional | none |
| use-case | forbidden | required ("supported") | optional | a goal |
| principle | required | forbidden | optional | a goal or use-case |
| constraint | required | forbidden | required: environment or tradeoff | a decision or component when tradeoff |
| requirement | required | required ("met") | optional | a goal or use-case |
| decision | required | required ("in effect") | optional | a principle, constraint, or requirement |
| component | forbidden | required ("exists") | optional | a decision or requirement |
| guide | required | forbidden | optional | a principle or decision |
| reference | forbidden | forbidden | forbidden | none; no intent either |

The why-chain reads bottom-up: components and decisions require norms (principles, constraints, requirements); norms require purposes (goals, use-cases); purposes require nothing. A norm with no goal or use-case above it is the loudest gardening signal. There is no `tier` field; the grouping is a property of the type definition.

The typing test (lives in the doc-types definition, not frontmatter): realizable + normative + a choice → decision; realizable, not normative → component; normative, not realizable → principle, or constraint when imposed from outside. Constraint is visibly requirement + external imposition + must; the type is kept for the vocabulary.

### Surfaces and rules
- Every doc line, text and JSON, on every surface (ls, find, map including children, neighbors, graph, log, review snapshots, hierarchy.md, MCP tools): `<Intent> <type>: <Title>` followed by force and realization tags; deprecated struck through with its successor; references omitted where they are omitted today and ranked last elsewhere.
- Ranking everywhere: status (living, deprecated, reference), then intent (requested, chosen, unattributed, incidental), then relevance, replacing oldest-first.
- Validate: intent above incidental without a basis (error); force or realization on a type that forbids it (error); realizable doc without realization (warning on existing docs); constraint without imposition (warning); `tradeoff` without an upstream decision or component (warning); deprecated without `superseded_by` (error, as today); expected why-chain missing (warning); obsolete `level` key or retired `target` value present (warning naming the migration).
- Queues that fall out: `ldoc ls --intent incidental` is the ratification queue; `ldoc ls --realization planned` is the backlog.

### Tooling posture
Loose on input, strict on output. Reading: unknown fields are preserved and ignored; expected-but-missing fields warn; everything keeps functioning. Writing: the tool refuses to write a doc that violates the table (e.g. `ldoc new --type decision` without `--force` and `--realization`; `--intent requested` without `--intent-basis`), failing loud with the instruction to fix it.

Migration of existing stores is a follow-up: a normalized, repeatable script or agentic process. Not part of this request beyond applying the new structure to this repo's own docs.

## C. Rulings made during the session
1. Three intent rungs only; no `proposed`, no `trial`, no ratified/mandated ladder.
2. Force is a separate facet from intent (rule vs guideline), RFC 2119 words.
3. Realization is its own field, not a type-conditioned status vocabulary.
4. No `tier` property. The why-chain is expressed as expected `requires` edges per type.
5. `imposed_by` with values environment / tradeoff / choice (name chosen by the agent after the owner rejected "agency"; owner has not objected).
6. No ADR/NFR template sections are added, enforced, or advised.
7. Loose on input, strict on output.
8. Migration tooling is a follow-up; this repo's docs are moved to the new structure now, by bulk edit where appropriate.
