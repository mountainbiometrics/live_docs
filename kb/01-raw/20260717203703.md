I ommitted "references" from a lot of views, cause they're not living documentation, and though we might want to keep them around for _referencing_; they're not the point of this system.
They're important as, and their "prominence" should be equivalent to, the backlink of a footnote.

When I deprioritized them, I was mostly looking at the "viewer" (cause that's what I use to look through livedocs), and didn't notice that the CLI didn't do the same cleanup (though I should have, after-all those were different items in my wishlist, and it's clear that only the one side was touched).  I noticed now when I checked to see what an agent would get if they ran `ldoc map` in my other project's store: and most of what I saw was a huge list of reference docs (because they were "global" cause they weren't "scoped", cause I didn't worry about organizing them because they're not important).  This is super problematic.
I had been thinking about this for a bit (as you can see here: WISH.md Reference handling — disregard in most ldoc commands like validate; deprioritize in search; disregard in viewer views; immutability).
but I had been thinking that those were just "nice to have" things; but I'm now realizing that this is actually critical for certain aspects of the CLI; because that determines what gets explored or learned by agents using this system.
