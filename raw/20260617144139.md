## The short version

The hierarchy idea holds its own — but not as "a tree." You're bundling **three genuinely separate needs** under one word, and a single tree structure would serve all three *badly* while reintroducing the exact maintenance cost that [index-summaries-v1](docs/20260615204138.md) consciously deferred. Untangle them and most of the tension dissolves. Two of the three don't even need a new structure.

## Your four felt-gaps map onto three needs

| You said | Underlying need | Is it a "tree"? |
|---|---|---|
| (1) No overviews without scanning everything | **Summaries / content rollups** | No — this is the v1 deferral, orthogonal to structure |
| (3) No signposts when search misses | **Browsable entry points (curation)** | Partly — this is what index docs are for |
| (2) Clusters are incidental | Split: sparse graph *(gardening)* + no first-class "these belong together" *(curation)* | Partly |
| (4) Sub-principle-of vs. similar-to vs. constrained-by | **Edge typing (relationship semantics)** | No — this is a schema gap, and it *subsumes* "parent/child" |

The thing worth internalizing: **"parent/child" is just one *type* of edge.** Right now every edge is untyped — `depends_on` means "cascade" and `references` means "provenance," and that's the entire vocabulary. So "hierarchy" isn't a missing *structure*; it's a missing *edge type* (`refines` / `parent-of`) plus the curation to lay those edges down. That reframes Need 4 as the keystone: solve edge-typing and the hierarchy view falls out as "project the subgraph of `refines` edges."

## Your "foundational vs. integral" instinct is sharp — and it's a *metric*, not a position

You nailed something: edge-count gives you "integral" (highly touched = a **hub**), but not "foundational." Those are different graph properties, and you can compute **both from the DAG you already have** — no tree required:

- **Integral** = high total degree. A hub everything brushes against.
- **Foundational** = high *transitive reverse-reachability* (lots of docs depend on it, directly or through chains) **and** low outbound depth (it rests on little itself). In your `depends_on` DAG these are the near-**sinks** that everything points back toward.
- **"Closeness to the trunk"** = longest-path depth from the root/source docs.

So the signal you hoped to *read off a tree's shape* is actually derivable as a rank over the existing graph. That's a much cheaper win than building and maintaining a tree — and it's more honest, because foundational-ness emerges from how the corpus actually wires itself, rather than from where you manually filed something.

## Does the tree hold its own?

A **strict single-parent tree**: no. Your own [original-idea-dump](docs/20260615203646.md) said "Hierarchy/**DAG**" — you always knew it was a DAG. A decision refines several principles; a constraint scopes several components. Single-parent filing will fight the content and you'll resent it.

A **navigable hierarchy *view*** (signpost/index docs + a `refines` edge you can render tree-ish): yes, absolutely. That's the signpost need, and it's real. The distinction matters: don't add a structural primitive, add an *edge type and a view over it*.

## So, my recommendation — in leverage order

1. **Edge typing — the keystone.** Give edges a small, *closed* vocabulary (I'd cap it brutally: `refines`, `constrains`, `relates`, on top of the existing cascade/provenance split). This directly solves Need 4, makes "parent/child" just `refines`, and lets `cascade-check` get smarter (a `relates` edge shouldn't cascade like a `refines` one). **Risk to name out loud:** edge taxonomies love to balloon into an ontology bikeshed — which would violate your Maintainability principle. The discipline is keeping it to ~3 types, forever.
   - *Plugs into:* `ldoc link/unlink/neighbors/graph`, `reindex` (typed reverse maps), `cascade-check` (type-aware verdicts), `validate`.

2. **Revisit the summaries deferral (Need 1).** You wrote the trigger condition into [index-summaries-v1](docs/20260615204138.md) yourself: "when the corpus grows large enough that the children table is insufficient... revisit." You're feeling that now. You even pre-named the skill: `summarize-descendants`.

3. **Surface foundational-ness as a computed rank.** No new structure — emit it from `reindex` and show it in `ldoc find`/`show`. This is probably the single highest ratio of "fixes the felt clunkiness" to "implementation cost."

4. **A gardening pass that proposes signposts/clusters (Need 2/3).** Detect dense regions or orphan-y neighborhoods and *propose* index docs. This is the "curation/gardening" you suspected was the real gap — and I think you're right that a chunk of the clunkiness is under-gardened edges, not missing structure.

My honest weighting of why exploration feels clunky: ~40% missing edges/curation, ~40% missing summaries, ~20% missing edge-types. Building a rigid tree addresses none of those cleanly and adds burden — which is why your hesitation is well-founded.

Strongly agree with the method — **name the type after its behavior, derive the set from the behavior matrix, then collapse identical rows.** Let me expand the dimensions first (you asked for more use-cases — I've got ~6 beyond your five), then build the matrix and constrain it back.

## The full list of "what does an edge actually *do*"

Your five:
1. Edit *this* → cascade-check the neighbor? (forward cascade)
2. Edit the *neighbor* → cascade-check this? (reverse cascade)
3. Remove the neighbor → orphan / slate-for-removal this? (removal jeopardy)
4. Updated summary propagates (which direction)? (rollup)
5. Used by graph traversal? (discovery)

Six more that are live or near-live in *your* system specifically:
6. **Does it define the browse/map hierarchy?** — i.e. does the neighbor show up in the signpost/`hierarchy.md` rollup as a child. This is the big one; it's the actual "structural" question and it cleaves the set.
7. **Does it feed the foundational/centrality rank?** (your integral-vs-foundational metric reads only certain edges)
8. **Is acyclicity enforced?** — the validator should reject cycles on *some* types (a tree spine) but allow them on others (the influence web). Cycle-legality is per-type, which is the concrete form of your "it's a web, not a DAG" fix.
9. **Lifecycle side-effect** — does asserting the edge mutate `status` (e.g. supersession flips the source to `historical`)?
10. **Can the target live outside the validated graph?** — `references` can point at `raw/` or an external URL or a historical doc; structural edges can't. This drives `validate`'s dangling rule per-type.
11. **garden / orphan / split behavior** — when a doc splits, which edges copy to both halves; and "orphan" stops being one thing (a doc rich in `relates` but with no structural edge is *navigable but unrooted* — a different finding than truly disconnected).

So edge-typing doesn't just answer Need 4 — it parameterizes `cascade-check`, `reindex`, `validate`, **and** `garden` all at once.

## The behavior matrix

Collapsing your candidate names into behavior profiles:

| type | dir | cascade | removal jeopardy | structural (map/rollup/rank) | discovery | lifecycle fx | target outside graph | acyclic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **part_of** | directed | both ways | yes (orphan) | **YES** | yes | no | no | yes |
| **requires** | directed | yes (esp. reverse) | **YES (slate for removal)** | no | yes | no | no | yes |
| **relates** | symmetric | no | no | no | **YES** | no | no | n/a |
| **references** | directed | no | no | no | yes | no | **YES** | yes |
| *superseded_by* (reserve) | directed | no | inverse* | no | yes | **YES → historical** | no | yes |

\* supersession doesn't orphan the source — it *keeps* it as `historical` and points to the successor.

Every row is a genuinely distinct profile. That's the test passing.

## The two collapses that constrain it back

**Collapse 1 — taxonomic vs. compositional is NOT a functional difference.** You floated splitting hierarchy into `refines` (is-a / sub-principle) vs `part_of` (has-a / the big decision composed of small ones). Run them through the matrix: *identical on every column* — both directed, both cascade both ways, both feed the rollup/rank, both acyclic, both orphan on parent-removal. By your own rule, identical behavior = one type. The is-a/has-a flavor is semantics for the body, not a second edge. → **one structural type.**

**Collapse 2 — the real cleaver is "structural or not," and it separates `part_of` from `requires`.** Both are directed and cascade. The difference is column 6: a sub-principle lives *under* its parent in the browse tree (structural — feeds map, rollup, rank). A principle *predicated on a constraint* does **not** live under the constraint in the map — they're different doc types in different regions — yet a change/removal must still cascade and jeopardize. That's a non-structural cascade link. Your own example proves it needs to exist: *"remove the constraint → the principle can be removed"* is column 3 firing with column 6 off. `relates` can't carry that (no cascade, no jeopardy), and `part_of` would wrongly file the principle under the constraint. So `requires` earns its slot.

## The constrained set (named for function)

Three new + the existing one + one reserved:

- **`part_of`** — structural spine. The only edge that feeds the hierarchy view, summary rollup, and foundational rank; cascades both ways; acyclic. *(alts: `belongs_to`, `composes`. reverse map: "parts"/the children list — this is your signpost rollup.)*
- **`requires`** — non-structural existential cascade. Strong reverse cascade + removal-jeopardy; does *not* touch the map. This is what `depends_on` should always have meant. *(alts: `predicated_on`, or reclaim `depends_on` — though reclaiming the term that caused the confusion invites it back. reverse: "required_by".)*
- **`relates`** — symmetric, no cascade, discovery/clustering only. *(alts: `similar_to`, `related`. reverse: itself.)*
- **`references`** — keep as-is. Provenance; no cascade; the one edge allowed to point outside the graph.
- **`superseded_by`** — *reserve the slot, build later.* It's the only one with a lifecycle side-effect (flips `status → historical`), so it wants its own handling — but you don't need it the moment you add the other three.

Migration falls out cleanly: today's `depends_on` edges are a blend of `part_of` and `requires`; `garden` retypes each one based on whether the neighbor is structurally "under" the doc or just required by it. `references` is untouched.

## Where you're right

You correctly spotted that "references" is a *connotative* name hiding its actual function — the exact sin we've been hunting ("dependency" → DAG, all over again). "References" sounds like a generic soft link, which is precisely what makes it *feel* collapsible into `relates`. And two of the matrix columns I used to distinguish them don't survive scrutiny:

- **Acyclic** — not load-bearing. Provenance is acyclic *incidentally* (you derive from what came before), not by an enforced rule. Concede it; drop the column.
- **Target-outside-graph** — not an *independent* axis. It's a *consequence* of the provenance semantics (attributing a living doc to its raw/external source), not a free property you could bolt onto `relates`. "This principle relates to a raw clipping" has no meaning — you cluster docs with docs. So it doesn't argue for two types on its own.

So if all that distinguished them were those two columns, you'd be right to collapse. And your proposed resolution is the correct one: **if we care about provenance, the edge should just be named `provenance`.** We do care — `ingest-reference` is built on it, `validate` enforces it, the `raw/` tier exists for it. So rename it. That makes the surviving distinction honest instead of smuggled-in under a vague word.

## Where they *don't* collapse — what actually survives the rename

Three functional differences remain, and none of them is cosmetic:

1. **Direction is the information.** Provenance is "A was derived from / attributed to B" — inherently asymmetric. The arrow *is* the content: B is the source, A the derivative. The `referenced_by` reverse map ("everything derived from this source") is only meaningful because of that direction. `relates` is symmetric — collapsing would erase the source/derivative ordering, which is the whole point of provenance.

2. **Immutable fact vs. living judgment.** Provenance records *what happened* at ingest — it doesn't change when the source changes and it survives the source going `historical`. `relates` is a *current* assessment ("these are kin right now"); if the docs drift apart you delete the edge. Functional consequence: **`garden` prunes stale `relates` edges but must never prune a provenance edge** (you can't un-derive something). They behave differently under maintenance.

3. **`validate` integrity rule.** Every doc extracted by `ingest-reference` is *required* to carry a provenance edge — `validate` enforces it. There is no analogous invariant for `relates`. One participates in structural integrity; the other is purely optional navigation.

So: same matrix *row* on the soft columns (no cascade, no jeopardy, discovery-only), but distinct on direction-as-meaning, mutability, and the integrity rule. That's enough to keep them — but you were right that the name had to change for the distinction to be defensible.

## Vocabulary — locked

| edge | function |
|---|---|
| **`belongs_to`** | structural spine — hierarchy view, summary rollup, foundational rank; cascades both ways; orphans on parent removal |
| **`requires`** | existential cascade — strong reverse cascade + removal-jeopardy; *not* structural |
| **`relates`** | symmetric, mutable, discovery/clustering only; garden prunes when stale |
| **`provenance`** (was `references`) | directed, immutable derivation fact; validate-enforced; may point outside the graph |

Four types, each a distinct behavior profile, every name earned by function. The `references → provenance` rename rides the `garden` field-alias pass, so it's a mechanical migration, not a big-bang.
