## Context

Ids are opaque (Kiste/Zettelkasten) and titles are descriptive but long, so neither is a friendly handle for humans or agents referencing docs in commands and skill steps.

## Decision

Every doc carries a short, unique `label` in addition to `title`. A label is non-empty, trimmed, unique across the store (case-insensitive), and matches `^[A-Za-z0-9]+([ -][A-Za-z0-9]+)*$` — letters and digits with single spaces or hyphens between tokens. Whitespace IS allowed, so `label-and-title` and `porcelain api` are both valid; kebab-case remains a special case. Resolution accepts id, exact label, exact title, or a unique case-insensitive substring of title or label — whichever the caller finds most natural. Multi-word labels are quoted on the CLI.

When `ldoc new` (or `KB.new`) is given no `--label`, one is auto-derived from the title: take whole words up to ~24 characters, breaking only on word boundaries (never mid-word), strip trailing punctuation. The result is **Title Case** (each word capitalised), NOT kebab-cased — inter-word spaces are preserved and the casing convention makes labels read as names, not slugs. On collision, ` 2`, ` 3`, … is appended.

### Label casing convention

A label is a short, human-readable **Title-Case** name — whitespace is allowed and words are capitalised. Examples: `Porcelain API`, `Batch Operations`, `Label and Title`. Kebab slugs (e.g. `porcelain-api`) are valid syntax but are the exceptional case, not the norm. Autogeneration always produces Title Case from word boundaries; hand-written labels should also follow Title Case. (Existing labels predate this convention and are not being retroactively renamed — that migration is a separate task.)

## Consequences

- Humans and agents reference docs by label in `ldoc` commands and prose.
- Display labels stay compact and human-readable (the label is scannable; the title is readable in full context).
- Titles stay descriptive without needing to be terse.
- Allowing whitespace means auto-generated labels read as natural phrases rather than hyphenated slugs.
- Title Case makes labels visually distinct from both ids and arbitrary prose, reducing ambiguity in summaries and review output.
