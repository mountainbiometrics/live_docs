## Decision

Reviewers read a change **summary** that states *intent*, not raw diffs — the same way a pull-request description, not the patch, is the primary review surface.

## Summary shape

The summary is a small structured document with up to three sections, each line naming the affected doc **by its label**:

```
## Additions
New <Type>: <label> — <one-line intent>

## Revisions
<label> — <what substantively changed and why>

## Minor Alterations
<label> — <reference/edge/cosmetic touch-up>
```

## Rationale

Raw diffs impose high cognitive load (see cognitive-load): a reviewer must reverse-engineer intent from text deltas across many files. An intent-level summary, grouped by the *kind* of change and keyed to labels, lets a reviewer grasp what a changeset means at a glance and decide where to look closely. This is what makes review of canonical docs (reviewable-official-docs) tractable rather than burdensome — and it is the lever for reducing review burden without skipping review.
