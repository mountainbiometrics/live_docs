## Decision

An **async inbox** decouples *capture* from *ingestion*. Raw material can be dropped into an inbox **instantly**, with no processing at capture time. A separate **batch ingest pass** drains the inbox **on demand**, so a queue of accumulated inputs is processed — and reviewed — together.

## Rationale

- **Capture must be frictionless** to actually happen. If ingestion (decomposition into single-responsibility docs) ran synchronously at capture time, the cost of dropping a thought or a link would be high enough to discourage capture, and knowledge would be lost. An instant drop-point removes that friction.
- **Batched draining serves liveness and review** (living-over-stale): material does not rot uncaptured, yet ingestion still applies the full decomposition discipline (ingest-gardening-core) when the pass runs. Processing the inbox as a batch also lets the resulting changes be summarized and reviewed as one coherent unit rather than piecemeal.

## Relationship

This decision establishes the *async, two-phase* model. The concrete drop-point and the draining pass are specified by the inbox-pipeline component.
